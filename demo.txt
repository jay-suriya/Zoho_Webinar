1.  WHAT A WEBINAR TOOL IS
================================================================================

SHOW
  Nothing yet. Do this one with the screen still on the CRM home page, or
  blank. It is ninety seconds of vocabulary and it works better without the
  mock competing for attention.

SAY
  Before we look at any screens, I want to make sure we are all holding the
  same picture of the thing we are integrating, because half of this room has
  probably never run a webinar.

  a webinar tool is an online software application used to host, manage,
  and broadcast virtual seminars, workshops, and presentations to a remote audience
  A webinar tool runs a scheduled online session for an audience. That is it.
  One or a few people present, and anywhere from hundreds to thousands people
  watch. It is different from a meeting. In a meeting everyone is a participant, everyone
  can talk, and you join by having the link. In a webinar the audience is an
  audience: they watch, they can ask questions in a panel, the anser for Polls.

  Make it concrete. Take Northline Capital - the business we are using all the
  way through today. They are wealth managers. Once a month they run a free
  market outlook webinar: an hour on where rates are going, open to anyone,
  no pitch until the last five minutes. Two hundred people sign up, sixty turn
  up, and the ones who stay behind asking what it means for their own money
  are the reason the webinar exists.

  They cannot cold-call their way to a portfolio - the industry does not allow
  it and nobody would take the call anyway. So they teach in public and let
  the interested identify themselves. The webinar is not marketing support for
  the sales motion, it is the entire top of it. Every question we ask for the
  rest of this hour is asked on Northline's behalf: who signed up, who
  actually came, and which of them became a client.

  That signing up is the part that matters to us, so let me walk the shape of
  it. A webinar has a date, a time and a host. It publishes a registration
  form. People fill it in, and at that moment they become a registrant. They
  get a confirmation email, then a reminder or two as the date gets closer. On
  the day, some of them turn up and some do not, and that split is the whole
  game: the ones who turned up are attendees, the ones who did not are
  absentees, and afterwards they get different emails - the attendees get a
  thank-you, the absentees get the recording.

  While the session is running, the tool captures things worth keeping. Polls
  the host launches and how the room answered. Questions people typed and what
  the host replied. A recording. Whatever files were shared.

  So the vocabulary for the next hour:

      Webinar        the scheduled session
      Registration   the form, and the act of signing up through it
      Registrant     someone who signed up
      Attendee       a registrant who turned up
      Absentee       a registrant who did not
      Polls / Q&A    what the room did during the session

  If you think of it as a state machine it is almost trivial - invited, then
  registered, then attended or not attended - and that is exactly why this is
  worth integrating. Those states are facts about a person, and we already have
  a system whose entire job is holding facts about people.

  [Pause here. Ask: "everyone comfortable with registrant versus attendee?"
   It is the distinction the rest of the session leans on, and it is the one
   people quietly get wrong.]

LAND
  A webinar tool turns strangers into registrants, and registrants into
  attendees or absentees. Those are facts about people - and people are what
  CRM is for.
