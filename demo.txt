================================================================================
ZOHO CRM  x  ZOHO WEBINAR  —  DEMO SCRIPT
Walkthrough narrative for the templates-flow mock (webinar.html)
================================================================================

HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT
  Seven acts, in the order you click through the mock. Each act has:
    SAY      - the words, spoken aloud
    SHOW     - what to have on screen
    LAND     - the one sentence the audience must walk away with
  Total runtime: about 18-20 minutes at a comfortable pace.
  Anything in [brackets] is a stage direction, not something you say.

THE BUSINESS WE'LL USE THROUGHOUT
  Bright Retail Systems. They sell billing-and-inventory software to small
  shops - grocers, pharmacies, hardware stores. Six people in sales.
  A deal is worth about $2,000. Nobody buys on the first call.
  Our person is JAY, a sales rep. He also happens to be the one who set up
  the CRM, which is true of most companies this size.

  Why this business: small, unglamorous, and every number in the demo is a
  number the audience can hold in their head. Keep coming back to Jay by name.
  He is the hero of this story, not the software.


BEFORE YOU PRESENT  (five minutes of prep, worth doing)
  1. The sample data is generic. The scheduled webinar in the mock is called
     "Real Estate Webinar" and that will contradict a billing-software story
     the moment it's on screen. Two options, pick one:
       - Edit it. Open the webinar, hit Edit, rename it to
         "Cut your billing errors in half" and save. Cleanest.
       - Or never read the title aloud. Say "Jay's Thursday session" and
         point at the numbers instead. Works fine; nobody reads titles
         unless you do.
     The completed one, "Product Reveal Webinar", already fits the story.
  2. Serve the mock and open it before the room fills up:
       python3 -m http.server 8090
       http://localhost:8090/webinar.html
     Add ?v=2 to the URL if you've been editing - the server sends no cache
     headers and Chrome will happily show you a stale copy.
  3. Click the whole path once, in order, before you present. Appendix A is
     the running order. The point is to find the one button that needs a
     second click, not to memorise anything.
  4. Have the numbers in Appendix B in front of you. You will be asked
     "what was the ROI again?" and hunting for it on screen kills the
     moment.

================================================================================
ACT 1  -  WHAT A WEBINAR IS, AND WHY JAY RUNS ONE
================================================================================

SHOW   Nothing yet. Slides off. Just talk. Ninety seconds, no screen.

SAY
  "Let's start with something ordinary. A webinar.

   Strip away the jargon and a webinar is a scheduled online session where
   one company talks and many people listen, ask questions, and leave their
   contact details on the way in.

   Who actually runs them? Not marketing departments at big companies -
   they're the ones everybody thinks of. In practice it's teams like Jay's.
   Six salespeople at Bright Retail Systems, selling billing software to
   small shops.

   Why does Jay run one? Do the arithmetic he does. He has 400 leads from
   ads and trade shows. He can call them one at a time - that's 400 calls
   to find the 30 who are actually shopping this quarter. Or he can run one
   session on Thursday, called 'Cut your billing errors in half', and let
   the 30 identify themselves by showing up.

   That's what a webinar really is for a team this size. It isn't marketing
   theatre. It's a filter. It's the cheapest way Jay has to find out which
   of his 400 names are worth a phone call."

  [Pause. Now the problem.]

  "So he runs it. And this is where it falls apart.

   Jay's webinar tool and Jay's CRM are two different products. So:

     One - He can't invite the people he actually wants. His leads live in
     the CRM. The webinar tool has no idea they exist. So he exports a CSV.
     Every single time.

     Two - The people who register are strangers to his CRM. Somebody signs
     up on Tuesday. That registration sits in the webinar tool. Jay's
     colleague calls the same person on Wednesday with no idea they just
     signed up for Thursday's session.

     Three - After the session he has an attendance list and no way to use
     it. Fifteen people attended, fifteen didn't. Those are two completely
     different sales conversations, and the list is in the wrong system.

     Four - And the question his manager actually asks: 'Did the webinar
     make us any money?' Jay cannot answer it. He has attendance in one
     tool and deals in another, and no thread between them. So he says
     'it went well' and everyone moves on.

   Notice what all four have in common. None of them is a webinar problem.
   Zoho Webinar hosts the session perfectly well. Every one of them is a
   problem of the webinar not knowing anything about the customer."

LAND
  "A webinar is how Jay finds out who's interested. The problem is that
   what he finds out is trapped in the wrong system."


================================================================================
ACT 2  -  WHY THIS INTEGRATION
================================================================================

SHOW   Still no screen. Thirty seconds. This act is one idea, said cleanly.

SAY
  "So the fix isn't a better webinar tool. It's making the webinar happen
   where the customer already lives - inside the CRM.

   Take the four problems, one at a time:

     Jay couldn't invite his own leads.
       -> Now he invites CRM records directly. No CSV, ever.

     Registrations were strangers.
       -> Now a registration lands on the CRM record. Anyone on the team
          who opens that lead sees it.

     The attendance list was unusable.
       -> Now attended and didn't-attend are two lists he can act on
          without leaving CRM.

     He couldn't prove it made money.
       -> Now the webinar is tied to the deals it influenced, with a
          revenue number on the webinar itself.

   And one more thing, the one Jay cares most about and would never say
   out loud: he doesn't have to learn a second product. He has never opened
   standalone Zoho Webinar and he doesn't want to. Everything you're about
   to see, he does from inside the CRM he already uses every day."

LAND
  "The webinar doesn't become a better webinar. It becomes part of the CRM."


================================================================================
ACT 3  -  TURNING IT ON  (the integration page)
================================================================================
[Open the mock. Setup (gear) -> Marketplace -> Zoho -> Zoho Webinar]

SHOW   The introduction panel, before enabling.

SAY
  "Here's the first design decision worth pointing out. When Jay picks
   Zoho Webinar, we don't ask him for anything yet. We explain ourselves
   first - what this makes possible, how it works, why teams turn it on.
   Enable Integration sits in the top right and waits.

   Small thing. But Jay is about to connect a product to his customer
   database. Being asked for credentials before being told what happens
   is how people quietly back out."

  [Click Enable Integration -> the setup page]

SAY
  "Now the setup, and there are only three things on it.

   First, the connection. His email, his organisation, and one question:
   sync the webinars he's already run? Say yes, and the webinars from
   before today come in with their attendance history. His reporting
   doesn't start from zero on day one."

  [Scroll to: Modules You Can Invite]

SAY
  "Second - and this is the one worth slowing down on - Modules You Can
   Invite.

   In CRM, people live in modules. Leads. Contacts. Vendors. Custom ones
   you built yourself. This setting answers one question: which of those
   can a webinar invite go to?

   Why ask at all? Two reasons, and they're both practical.

     One is relevance. Jay invites Leads and Contacts. He is never going
     to invite a Vendor to a customer webinar - those are his suppliers.
     If Vendors stays on, it clutters every invite he ever sends, forever.

     The other is safety. Whatever you turn on here is what appears in the
     module dropdown when someone clicks Send Invite. Turn something off
     here and it's gone from there. So this is the guard rail: it decides
     who your team can email before anyone is in a hurry.

   That's why it's a setup decision and not a per-webinar one. You choose
   the audience once, calmly, and the daily flow inherits it."

  [Scroll to: Webinar Deal Attribution]

SAY
  "Third, Webinar Deal Attribution. And this is the one that answers Jay's
   manager.

   Here's the situation it exists for. Priya attends three of Jay's
   webinars over two months. Then she buys - a $3,000 deal. Which webinar
   gets the credit?

   It's a real question, not an academic one, because next quarter Jay has
   to decide whether to keep running these. If all three webinars each claim
   the full $3,000, he thinks he made $9,000 from one $3,000 sale. Attribution
   is what stops you lying to yourself.

   So you pick a rule, once:

     First Interaction   - all credit to the webinar that found her.
                           Use it when you care about what generates demand.
     Last Interaction    - all credit to the one just before she bought.
                           Use it when you care about what closes.
     Linear              - split it evenly across all three. The honest
                           default when you don't know. It's what we
                           pre-select.
     Time Decay          - the closer to the sale, the more credit.
                           Increasing or decreasing.
     Position-Based      - most credit to the first and the last, less to
                           the middle.

   We default to Linear on purpose. It's the one that makes the fewest
   claims. Jay can change it when he has an opinion; until then he isn't
   accidentally over-crediting anything."

LAND
  "Two decisions, made once: who you can invite, and how credit gets
   counted. Everything after this is Jay just doing his job."


================================================================================
ACT 4  -  CREATING THE WEBINAR
================================================================================
[Zoho Webinar module -> Create Webinar]
[KEEP THIS SHORT. Two minutes. Do not walk the form field by field.]

SHOW   The Create Webinar form. Scroll it once, top to bottom, fairly briskly.

SAY
  "Jay creates the webinar. Title, date and time, timezone, how long,
   who's hosting.

   I'm going to move through this quickly, because the point isn't the form.
   The point is that it's a CRM form. Same layout, same fields on the left,
   same Save in the corner as every other record he creates. Nobody has to
   train him on this screen.

   Two things to notice as I scroll, and then we'll move on.

   Registration setup - whether people register, whether he approves them
   himself or it's automatic, whether there's a cap on seats.

   And the emails. Confirmation, reminders, follow-ups - already filled in
   with sensible defaults, so a webinar Jay creates in ninety seconds still
   sends a proper confirmation and a proper reminder. He can change them.
   He mostly won't. That's deliberate: the defaults are there so that doing
   nothing still produces something professional.

   He hits Save. The webinar is scheduled. Now the interesting part starts."

LAND
  "Creating it looks like creating any CRM record, which is exactly the point."


================================================================================
ACT 5  -  THE SCHEDULED WEBINAR  (the working screen)
================================================================================
[Zoho Webinar -> Real Estate Webinar (Scheduled)]

SHOW   The scheduled webinar detail page.

SAY
  "This is the screen Jay will actually live on for the next two weeks.
   And there are only two questions in his head when he opens it:
   'how many have signed up' and 'how do I get more'.

   Let's take them in that order."

  [Point at the header]

SAY
  "First, the header. Notice it's honest about where we are in time -
   'Starts in 16 minutes'. And the actions are the three things he'd want:
   Start Webinar, Send Invite, View Registration Link."

  [Point at Registration Overview]

SAY
  "Registrations: 10 of 200, with 190 seats left. The ring shows how full
   he is. And 5 waiting for approval - he chose manual moderation, so five
   people have asked to come in and need a yes. That's a click, from here."

  [Point at Registration by Module]

SAY
  "Where they came from, in CRM terms - 7 Leads, 2 Contacts, 1 Vendor.
   This is a view standalone webinar software simply cannot produce, because
   it doesn't know what a Lead is. Jay reads it as 'this is filling up with
   new prospects, not just existing customers' - which for a webinar meant
   to generate pipeline is exactly what he wants to see."

  [Point at Registration by Source]

SAY
  "Where they came from on the internet - email, LinkedIn, Twitter, direct.
   Two bars per channel: how many visited, how many actually registered.
   That gap is the interesting part. A channel that sends lots of visitors
   who don't sign up is telling you the click was curious but the offer
   didn't land.

   And these numbers exist because of the registration link."

  [Click View Registration Link. Show the modal. Toggle Source Tracking on.]

SAY
  "One link he can copy anywhere, plus embed code to put it on the website.
   And source tracking - he creates a separate link per channel, and each
   one reports visits and registrations back. That's how the chart we just
   looked at gets filled in."

  [Point at Daily Registration Trend]

SAY
  "And the shape over time. Peak day, average per day. Jay uses this to
   decide whether to push again this week - a flat line for three days is
   a nudge to go send more invites."

  [Scroll to Registrants list]

SAY
  "Then the people themselves. Approved, unapproved, denied - the moderation
   queue. Names, emails, which module, which source, when they registered.

   And notice: these are CRM records. He can click through to the lead."

  [Scroll to the Invited panel]

SAY
  "And here's the one Jay works from. He invited 100 people. This splits
   them: registered, and not registered.

   That second list is the most valuable thing on this page. Those are
   people who already got an email from him and haven't signed up. They're
   warm, they're identified, and they're one reminder away. In his old
   world that list didn't exist - he had an invite list in a CSV and a
   registration list in another tool and no way to subtract one from the
   other."

  [Click Send Invite briefly - show the record picker, then Mass Email]

SAY
  "And when he wants more, Send Invite. He picks the module - and only the
   modules we turned on in setup appear here, which is that guard rail
   doing its job. He picks records the way he picks records anywhere in CRM,
   with his own views and filters. Then it becomes a mass email with the
   invitation template already chosen.

   No export. No import. He never left CRM."

LAND
  "Before the webinar, this page answers two questions: how many, and who
   do I chase next. The second one is a list of names he can act on today."

ACT 6  -  THE COMPLETED WEBINAR  (handling the attendees)
================================================================================
[Zoho Webinar -> Product Reveal Webinar (Completed)]

SHOW   The completed webinar detail page.

SAY
  "The webinar has happened. Same record, different job. Before, Jay was
   trying to fill seats. Now he's trying to turn a room full of people
   into conversations.

   Look at the top line: 30 registered, 15 attended, 15 didn't. Fifty-fifty,
   which is normal and worth saying out loud so nobody thinks it's a
   failure.

   But here's the thing - those two fifteens are not one list. They are
   two completely different sales situations."

  [Point at the two numbers as you say this]

SAY
  "The 15 who attended: they gave you an hour of their attention. They know
   what the product does. Jay's job is to call them while it's fresh.

   The 15 who didn't: they raised their hand and then something came up.
   They are still interested - they registered! They just need the recording
   and another chance. Completely different email, completely different call.

   Sending those two groups the same follow-up is the single most common
   mistake in webinars, and this screen makes it hard to do."

  [Point at the four pies]

SAY
  "Then attendance broken down the same two ways as registration - by module
   and by source. And this is where it gets genuinely useful, because now
   you can compare.

   Look: LinkedIn brought lots of registrations. But did LinkedIn people
   actually show up? Compare the source pies and you find out. A channel
   that registers well and attends badly is a channel bringing you tyre-
   kickers. Jay can only see that because both pies are on one screen."

  [Scroll to Attendees and Registrants List. Point at the Polls and Q&A columns.]

SAY
  "Now the list, and I want to draw your eye to two columns most people
   would scroll past: Polls and Q&A.

   Arjun answered 2 polls and asked a question. Karthik answered 1.
   Priya attended and did neither.

   That is a priority order. Somebody who typed a question out loud in
   front of strangers is more interested than somebody who left the tab
   open. Jay has 15 attendees and time to call maybe six today. This column
   tells him which six.

   Attendance is a yes-or-no. Engagement is a ranking. The ranking is worth
   more."

  [Scroll to Webinar Revenue and expand it]

SAY
  "And finally, the section that answers the question Jay could never answer.

   Webinar ROI. The webinar cost $2,500 to run. Deals won that this webinar
   is credited with: $16,800. That's a 572% return. There's another $32,450
   still in the pipeline. Cost per attendee $167, revenue per attendee $1,120.

   And it's not a claim - it's itemised. Here are the deals. Names, amounts,
   stage, the account, the contact. Jayas Enterprises, $1,000. Meena Cloud,
   $4,500. Seven of them. Jay's manager can click any one and land on the
   deal record.

   Pipeline by stage, so he can see what's still moving. And deals won by
   source, which closes the loop all the way back: Twitter brought four of
   the deals we won. That is a genuinely different fact from 'Twitter brought
   the most registrations', and it's the one that should decide next quarter's
   budget."

  [Optional, if you have time - scroll to Recordings / related lists]

SAY
  "And the rest is where you'd expect it: the recording, the polls, the
   questions, the files, and open activities - the follow-up tasks Jay
   created off the back of this session, sitting on the webinar record."

LAND
  "After the webinar this page does two things. It sorts a room full of
   people into who to call first. And it puts a revenue number on the
   webinar - which is what gets you permission to run the next one."


================================================================================
ACT 7  -  CLOSING
================================================================================

SHOW   Come off the screen. Look at the room.

SAY
  "Let's go back to where we started.

   Jay had four problems. He couldn't invite his own leads. Registrations
   were strangers to his CRM. His attendance list was unusable. And he
   couldn't prove any of it made money.

   Walk back through what we just saw:

     He invited CRM records directly - no CSV.
     Every registration landed on the record, for the whole team to see.
     Attended and didn't-attend became two lists he could act on, ranked
       by who actually engaged.
     And the webinar carries its own revenue number, itemised down to the
       individual deal.

   And the through-line: at no point did Jay open a second product. He
   created a webinar, filled it, ran it, and followed it up, from inside
   the CRM he was already in.

   That's the whole idea. Not a webinar tool bolted onto a CRM. A webinar
   that has become one more thing the CRM knows about your customer."

APPENDIX C  -  QUESTIONS YOU WILL GET
================================================================================
  "Do we have to use the CRM to run a webinar now?"
     No. This is for teams whose audience already lives in the CRM. If
     you're running a 5,000-person public conference, that's a different
     job and standalone Zoho Webinar is still the right tool.

  "What if someone registers who isn't in our CRM?"
     They come in through the public registration link and can be pushed
     into CRM as a new record - that's the Push to CRM setting on the
     webinar. Which is worth saying plainly: the webinar becomes a lead
     source, not just a lead filter.

  "Why is Linear the default attribution model?"
     Because it's the one that makes the fewest assumptions. First and Last
     Interaction both throw away information. Linear splits it evenly until
     you have a reason to believe otherwise.

  "Can I stop the team inviting the wrong people?"
     That's exactly what Modules You Can Invite is for. What you turn off
     there disappears from the Send Invite dropdown for everyone.

  "How is this different from just sending a campaign?"
     A campaign tells you who opened an email. This tells you who gave you
     an hour, what they asked about, and which deals it turned into.

  IF SOMETHING IN THE MOCK MISBEHAVES
     Say "this is a prototype, that's a known gap" and move on. Do not
     debug on stage. The story survives a broken button; it does not
     survive five minutes of silence.
