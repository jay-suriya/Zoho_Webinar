Walkthrough narrative for the templates-flow mock (webinar.html)

HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT
  Seven acts, in the order you click through the mock. Each act has:
    SAY      - the words, spoken aloud
    SHOW     - what to have on screen
    LAND     - the one sentence the audience must walk away with
  Total runtime: about 18-20 minutes at a comfortable pace.
  Anything in [brackets] is a stage direction, not something you say.

THE BUSINESS WE'LL USE THROUGHOUT
  Jay is a fitness coach. 180,000 followers across Instagram and YouTube,
  and behind the content is a real business: online coaching programmes.

    8-Week Kickstart          $1,000
    12-Week Transformation    $2,800
    1:1 Elite coaching        $4,500

  Two junior coaches work with him and one assistant. The way he sells is
  the way everyone in this industry sells: a free masterclass, and at the
  end of it, an offer.

  Why this business: everyone in the room understands it instantly, and
  the numbers in the mock are exactly the numbers this business produces.
  A deal is a person signing up for a programme. Ad spend is his biggest
  cost and he decides on it weekly - which makes the ROI section at the
  end land much harder than it would for a company with an annual budget.

  Keep coming back to Jay by name. He is the hero of this story, not the
  software.


BEFORE YOU PRESENT  (five minutes of prep, worth doing)
  1. ONE THING IN THE MOCK CONTRADICTS THIS STORY - read this before you
     stand up. On the completed webinar, the Deals Won table has an
     "Account Name" column showing company names: "Jayas Co", "Suriya
     Ltd", "Meena Tech". Jay coaches individuals, so there are no
     companies. Three ways to deal with it, pick one:
       - Best: rename them in the mock first. Open a few of those deals
         and change the deal names to programme names - "Priya - 12-Week
         Transformation", "Arjun - Elite 1:1". Ten minutes, and the table
         becomes the strongest slide you have.
       - Fine: don't read the table row by row. Point at the total and the
         range - "seven people signed up, $1,000 to $4,500 each" - and
         move to the source pie. Nobody reads columns you don't read.
       - If someone asks: say it straight. "CRM keeps companies and people
         in separate modules. For a coach every deal sits on a person, and
         the Account column is just empty. This is sample data."
     Do not discover this live.

  2. The scheduled webinar in the mock is called "Real Estate Webinar".
     Rename it - Edit, then something like "Why your workouts stopped
     working" - or simply never read the title aloud and say "Thursday's
     masterclass" instead. The completed one is "Product Reveal Webinar",
     which is close enough to a programme launch to leave alone.

  3. Serve the mock and open it before the room fills up:
       python3 -m http.server 8090
       http://localhost:8090/webinar.html
     Add ?v=2 to the URL if you have been editing - the server sends no
     cache headers and Chrome will happily show you a stale copy.

  4. Click the whole path once, in order, before you present. Appendix A
     is the running order. The point is to find the one button that needs
     a second click, not to memorise anything.

  5. Have the numbers in Appendix B in front of you. You will be asked
     "what was the ROI again?" and hunting for it on screen kills the
     moment.

ACT 1  -  WHAT A WEBINAR IS, AND WHY JAY RUNS ONE
================================================================================

SHOW   Nothing yet. Slides off. Just talk. Ninety seconds, no screen.

SAY
  "Let's start with something ordinary. A webinar.

   Strip away the jargon and a webinar is a scheduled online session where
   one person talks and many people listen, ask questions, and leave their
   contact details on the way in.

   Who actually runs them? Everybody pictures a software company doing a
   product demo. In practice, some of the heaviest users are people like
   Jay. Jay is a fitness coach. 180,000 followers, and behind the content
   a real business - coaching programmes from a thousand dollars to
   forty-five hundred.

   Why does Jay run a webinar? Do the arithmetic he does.

   He has 400 people who downloaded his free meal plan last month. Four
   hundred names. He could message them one at a time, and spend a week
   finding the twenty who are actually ready to pay for coaching. Or he
   runs one free masterclass on Thursday - 'Why your workouts stopped
   working' - and lets those twenty identify themselves by turning up.

   That is what a webinar really is for a business like his. It is not
   content. It is a filter. It is the cheapest way he has of finding out
   which of those 400 names is worth an hour of his time.

   And it is how the whole coaching industry sells: free masterclass, and
   at the end, an offer."

  [Pause. Now the problem.]

  "So he runs it. And this is where it falls apart.

   Jay's webinar tool and Jay's CRM are two different products. So:

     One - He can't invite the people he actually wants. His leads - the
     meal-plan downloads, the DMs, the people who filled his enquiry form
     - all live in the CRM. The webinar tool has no idea they exist. So he
     exports a CSV. Every single time.

     Two - The people who register are strangers to his CRM. Someone signs
     up on Tuesday. That registration sits in the webinar tool. On
     Wednesday one of his junior coaches DMs the same person a cold
     'interested in coaching?' message, with no idea they already
     registered for Thursday.

     Three - After the session he has an attendance list and no way to use
     it. Fifteen attended, fifteen didn't. Those two groups need
     completely different messages, and the list is in the wrong system.

     Four - And the one that actually costs him money: he spent $2,500 on
     ads to fill that masterclass. Did it pay for itself? He has
     attendance in one tool and sales in another and no thread between
     them. So he guesses. And next week he decides his ad budget on a
     guess.

   Notice what all four have in common. None of them is a webinar problem.
   Zoho Webinar hosts the session perfectly well. Every one of them is the
   webinar not knowing anything about the customer."

LAND
  "A webinar is how Jay finds out who's ready to buy. The problem is that
   what he finds out is trapped in the wrong system."


================================================================================
ACT 2  -  WHY THIS INTEGRATION
================================================================================

SHOW   Still no screen. Thirty seconds. This act is one idea, said cleanly.

SAY
  "So the fix isn't a better webinar tool. It's making the webinar happen
   where the customer already lives - inside the CRM.

   Take the four problems, one at a time:

     He couldn't invite his own leads.
       -> Now he invites CRM records directly. No CSV, ever.

     Registrations were strangers.
       -> Now a registration lands on the person's CRM record. His junior
          coach opens that lead and sees it before sending anything.

     The attendance list was unusable.
       -> Now attended and didn't-attend are two lists he can act on
          without leaving CRM.

     He couldn't tell whether the ad spend paid.
       -> Now the masterclass is tied to the programmes it sold, with a
          revenue number sitting on the webinar itself.

   And one more, the one Jay cares most about and would never say out
   loud: he doesn't want to learn a second product. He is a coach, not a
   systems person. Everything you're about to see, he does from inside the
   CRM he already uses."

LAND
  "The webinar doesn't become a better webinar. It becomes part of the CRM."

ACT 3  -  TURNING IT ON  (the integration page)
================================================================================
[Open the mock. Setup (gear) -> Marketplace -> Zoho -> Zoho Webinar]

SHOW   The introduction panel, before enabling.

SAY
  "Here's the first design decision worth pointing out. When Jay picks
   Zoho Webinar, we don't ask him for anything yet. We explain ourselves
   first - what this makes possible, how it works, why teams turn it on.
   Enable Integration sits in the top right and waits.

   Small thing. But Jay is about to connect a product to his customer
   database. Being asked for credentials before being told what happens
   is how people quietly back out."

  [Click Enable Integration -> the setup page]

SAY
  "Now the setup, and there are only three things on it.

   First, the connection. His email, his organisation, and one question:
   sync the webinars he's already run? Say yes, and the webinars from
   before today come in with their attendance history. His reporting
   doesn't start from zero on day one."

  [Scroll to: Modules You Can Invite]

SAY
  "Second - and this is the one worth slowing down on - Modules You Can
   Invite.

   In CRM, people live in modules. Leads. Contacts. Vendors. Custom ones
   you built yourself. This setting answers one question: which of those
   can a webinar invite go to?

   Why ask at all? Two reasons, and they're both practical.

     One is relevance. Jay invites Leads - the meal-plan downloads, the
     enquiries - and Contacts, who are his current and past clients. He is
     never going to invite a Vendor to a client masterclass: his Vendors
     are his supplement partner, his apparel sponsor and his video editor.
     Leave Vendors switched on and that list clutters every invite he
     sends, forever, and one day somebody picks it by accident.

     The other is safety. Whatever you turn on here is what appears in the
     module dropdown when someone clicks Send Invite. Turn something off
     here and it's gone from there. So this is the guard rail: it decides
     who your team can email before anyone is in a hurry.

   That's why it's a setup decision and not a per-webinar one. You choose
   the audience once, calmly, and the daily flow inherits it."

  [Scroll to: Webinar Deal Attribution]

SAY
  "Third, Webinar Deal Attribution. And this is the one that answers Jay's
   manager.

   Here's the situation it exists for. Priya watches three of Jay's
   masterclasses over two months. Then she signs up for the 12-Week
   Transformation - $2,800. Which masterclass gets the credit?

   This is not an academic question for Jay, it's a budget question he
   answers every week. If all three masterclasses each claim the full
   $2,800, his dashboard tells him he made $8,400 from one $2,800 sale.
   He then triples his ad spend on the back of a number that was never
   real. Attribution is what stops you lying to yourself with your own
   reporting.

   So you pick a rule, once:

     First Interaction   - all credit to the masterclass that found her.
                           Use it when you're judging which ad brings
                           new people in.
     Last Interaction    - all credit to the one just before she bought.
                           Use it when you're judging which session
                           actually converts.
     Linear              - split it evenly across all three. The honest
                           default when you don't know. It's what we
                           pre-select.
     Time Decay          - the closer to the sale, the more credit.
                           Increasing or decreasing.
     Position-Based      - most credit to the first and the last, less to
                           the middle.

   We default to Linear on purpose. It's the one that makes the fewest
   claims. Jay can change it when he has an opinion; until then he isn't
   accidentally over-crediting anything."

LAND
  "Two decisions, made once: who you can invite, and how credit gets
   counted. Everything after this is Jay just doing his job."

ACT 4  -  CREATING THE WEBINAR
================================================================================
[Zoho Webinar module -> Create Webinar]
[KEEP THIS SHORT. Two minutes. Do not walk the form field by field.]

SHOW   The Create Webinar form. Scroll it once, top to bottom, fairly briskly.

SAY
  "Jay creates the masterclass. Title, date and time, timezone, how long,
   who's hosting.

   I'm going to move through this quickly, because the point isn't the form.
   The point is that it's a CRM form. Same layout, same fields on the left,
   same Save in the corner as every other record he creates. Nobody has to
   train him on this screen.

   Two things to notice as I scroll, and then we'll move on.

   Registration setup - whether people register, whether he approves them
   himself or it's automatic, whether there's a cap on seats.

   And the emails. Confirmation, reminders, follow-ups - already filled in
   with sensible defaults, so a masterclass Jay sets up in ninety seconds
   between clients still sends a proper confirmation and a proper reminder.
   He can change them. He mostly won't. That's deliberate: the defaults are
   there so that doing nothing still produces something professional. For a
   one-person business that matters more than any feature on this page.

   He hits Save. The webinar is scheduled. Now the interesting part starts."

LAND
  "Creating it looks like creating any CRM record, which is exactly the point."

APPENDIX C  -  QUESTIONS YOU WILL GET
================================================================================
  "Do we have to use the CRM to run a webinar now?"
     No. This is for anyone whose audience already lives in the CRM. If
     you're running a 5,000-person public conference, that's a different
     job and standalone Zoho Webinar is still the right tool.

  "Would a solo coach really buy a CRM?"
     Fair question, and the honest answer is: they already have one, they
     just call it a spreadsheet. The moment there are two coaches messaging
     the same leads, the spreadsheet breaks. That's the buying moment, and
     it's exactly when webinar-and-CRM-in-one-place stops being a nicety.

  "What if someone registers who isn't in the CRM?"
     They come in through the public registration link and can be pushed
     into CRM as a new record - that's the Push to CRM setting on the
     webinar. Worth saying plainly, because for a coach it's the main way
     it works: a masterclass promoted on Instagram brings in people he has
     never met. So the webinar isn't only a filter for leads he already
     has. It's a lead source in its own right.

  "Why is Linear the default attribution model?"
     Because it's the one that makes the fewest assumptions. First and Last
     Interaction both throw away information. Linear splits it evenly until
     you have a reason to believe otherwise.

  "Can I stop the team inviting the wrong people?"
     That's exactly what Modules You Can Invite is for. What you turn off
     there disappears from the Send Invite dropdown for everyone.

  "How is this different from just sending a campaign?"
     A campaign tells you who opened an email. This tells you who gave you
     an hour of their evening, what they were worried about in their own
     words, and which programmes it sold.

  IF SOMETHING IN THE MOCK MISBEHAVES
     Say "this is a prototype, that's a known gap" and move on. Do not
     debug on stage. The story survives a broken button; it does not
     survive five minutes of silence.
